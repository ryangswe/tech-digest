## 🏆 Top 3

### 1. Stale CLAUDE.md files can override what the code actually says

A paper measures how coding agents follow a project's instruction file, such as CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md, even when it disagrees with the existing code and even when the agent notices the disagreement. It then tests whether a single sentence added to the user's own instruction file makes agents follow the code instead.

**Why it matters to you:** What belongs in CLAUDE.md is on your list, and this is a measured look at what happens when the file goes out of date.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30571)

### 2. Coding-agent benchmarks can demand details the task never states

The paper notes that coding-agent benchmarks describe a task in natural language but grade it with tests taken from the upstream project. Passing those tests can require information the description never states, such as a constant's name, a module path or an exact message. The authors call this a specification-oracle gap.

**Why it matters to you:** Results that question a benchmark are exactly what your evals interest asks for.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30635)

### 3. Kaggle's benchmark lead on why most AI benchmarks are broken

Janie Gu of Google DeepMind, who leads Kaggle Benchmarks, explains why most AI benchmarks are broken: they don't reflect real work, they're expensive to keep current, and their scores can't be verified. The talk covers Kaggle's reproduction of 65 industry benchmarks.

**Why it matters to you:** Another view on how to trust agent evaluations, which sits under your evals-for-coding-agents interest.

[AI Engineer (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aVbRSIQO2dE)
